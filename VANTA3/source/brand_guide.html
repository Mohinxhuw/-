Why these colours

Violet signals intelligence and premium positioning without the sameness of corporate blue; aqua reads as growth and trust; coral provides one confident point of emphasis. The vivid hues sit on a cool, near-white mist and a deep night tone, so contrast stays high without harshness.

Contrast (WCAG 2.1)

Ink on white 17.0:1 · Violet on white 6.1:1 · Aqua-text on white 5.2:1 · Coral-text on white 4.8:1 · Saffron-text on white 4.9:1 · Saffron on Night 10.1:1. Chart labels switch automatically to Ink where white text would fall below 4.5:1.

Cambria and Calibri ship with Microsoft Office on Windows and Mac and are available in PowerPoint for the web, so the deck renders without font embedding or substitution.

Grid & spacing
  • 16:9 widescreen, 13.33 × 7.5 in. Outer margin 0.6 in on all sides.
  • Content zone 12.13 in wide, from 1.72 in (below the title) to 6.78 in.
  • Standard gutter 0.3 in between cards; 2, 3 and 4-column splits.
  • Title block: kicker at 0.38 in, title at 0.72 in — identical on every content slide.
  • Footer line and slide number on the layout, 9 pt, never edited per slide.
Shapes & components
  • Cards: white, 0.1 in corner radius, soft 9% violet-tinted shadow.
  • Insight panels: Lavender-tint fill, no shadow, serif heading.
  • Icons: Lucide line icons inside tinted circles.
  • Signature motif: orbit rings and a coral “signal dot”.
  • No accent stripes, no underlines beneath titles.

03 · Data visualisation

Charts are native, editable and quiet

Series order
  • Every chart is a native PowerPoint chart with its data embedded (right-click › Edit Data).
  • Light horizontal gridlines (#E7E5F3), no vertical gridlines, no chart borders.
  • Data labels replace axes where precise values matter.
  • Highlight one category in Violet or Coral; keep context in Lavender.
  • Prior period in Lavender, current period in Violet.
  • Waterfall, funnel and range charts use a hidden helper series — keep it invisible.
  • State units in the chart heading (“$M”, “%”) and mark forecasts with “F”.

53 charts across 13 forms: column, bar, stacked, waterfall, funnel, line, area, combo, donut, radar, scatter, bubble and tornado.

04 · Motion

Animation principles

ElementEffectTiming
Kicker + titleFade, with slide entry0.5 s, starts immediately
Cards & panelsFade or Wipe from left / right0.35–0.5 s, 90–150 ms stagger
Column chartsWipe from bottom (bars grow)0.9–1.2 s
Bar, line, area, donut chartsWipe from left (reveals in reading order)0.9–1.8 s
Scatter, bubble, radarSubtle Zoom (60% → 100%) with fade0.6 s
TablesWipe from top0.6–0.9 s
KPI hero numbersSubtle Zoom0.45–0.6 s
Timelines & GanttAxis wipes first, then items step by step0.3–0.5 s per item
Slide transitionFade (content) · Push up (chapters)Medium / slow
Rules
  • Builds play automatically after the slide appears (“With / After previous”), so the deck runs without extra clicks. Most slides finish within 3 seconds, and none takes longer than 5.2 seconds.
  • Order follows the argument: headline → evidence (chart) → interpretation (insight panel).
  • Only four effects: Fade, Wipe, Zoom (subtle) and Appear. No bounce, spin, fly-in or emphasis loops.
  • Each object enters once; footers, layout art and slide numbers never animate.
  • Charts animate as a whole so they stay fully editable. For a series-by-series build, use Animation Pane › Effect Options › Sequence › By Series.
  • To present without motion, use Slide Show › Set Up Slide Show › Show without animation.

05 · Usage

Do and don't

Do

Write action titles that state the finding (“Asia-Pacific is our fastest-growing large region”), not topics (“Regional data”).

Don't

Repeat a title, or end a title with a full stop.

Do

Keep one dominant colour per slide and use Coral for a single point of emphasis.

Don't

Introduce new hues, gradients or stock-photo backgrounds.

Do

Build visuals from editable shapes, icons, charts and typography.

Don't

Use any image of people — photographs, portraits, silhouettes, AI-generated figures or illustrated characters.

Do

Edit numbers in the chart's data sheet so labels and bars stay in sync.

Don't

Paste charts as pictures or type values over a chart.

Do

Keep body text at 13 pt or larger and captions at 10 pt or larger.

Don't

Shrink text to fit — split the content across two slides instead.

Do

Label illustrative figures clearly; the footer reads “Illustrative data” until you replace it.

Don't

Present illustrative figures as actual results.
